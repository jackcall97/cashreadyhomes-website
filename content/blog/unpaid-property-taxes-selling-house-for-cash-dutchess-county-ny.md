Falling behind on property taxes happens more often than people think: a job change, a medical bill, an inherited house that came with its tax bills, or a paid-off mortgage that took the tax escrow with it. If you own a house in Dutchess County with back taxes, you still have options, and selling is one of them.

Here is the short answer: **when you sell a house with unpaid property taxes in New York, the back taxes, interest, and penalties are normally paid out of the sale proceeds at closing.** The title search finds them, the title company calculates the payoff, and the tax lien is cleared before the deed transfers. You walk away with whatever is left after taxes, any mortgage, and closing costs. The risk is waiting too long, because unpaid taxes can eventually lead to a tax foreclosure, where the county takes the property.

Tax rules and deadlines change, so confirm the numbers for your parcel with the tax office and your attorney.

## How property taxes are collected in Dutchess County

Most Dutchess County homeowners get more than one tax bill a year. County and town taxes are billed together and paid to your town's tax collector or receiver, and school taxes are billed separately by your school district. Village residents may also get a village tax bill.

According to the Dutchess County Commissioner of Finance, county and town taxes can be paid without penalty by **February 28** to the town tax collector or receiver (some towns extend this for seniors on Social Security). After the town's collection period ends, unpaid taxes are turned over to the county, and **delinquent taxes become payable to the Dutchess County Commissioner of Finance**, not your town.

There is one important exception. The county's Commissioner of Finance enforces delinquent taxes everywhere in Dutchess **except inside the cities of Poughkeepsie and Beacon**, which handle their own tax collection and enforcement. If your house is in the [City of Poughkeepsie](/sell-my-house-fast-poughkeepsie-ny) or [Beacon](/sell-my-house-fast-beacon-ny), check with the city about your balance. If it's in the Town of Poughkeepsie, [Wappingers Falls](/sell-my-house-fast-wappingers-falls-ny), [Fishkill](/sell-my-house-fast-fishkill-ny), [Hyde Park](/sell-my-house-fast-hyde-park-ny), [Rhinebeck](/sell-my-house-fast-rhinebeck-ny), [Red Hook](/sell-my-house-fast-red-hook-ny), [Millbrook](/sell-my-house-fast-millbrook-ny), or elsewhere in the county, the county is usually who you'll deal with.

## What happens if property taxes stay unpaid

In New York, unpaid real property taxes become a lien on the house. Interest and penalties are added over time, so the amount owed grows the longer it sits.

Counties enforce that lien under Article 11 of the New York Real Property Tax Law through a court process called an *in rem* tax foreclosure. In Dutchess County, the Commissioner of Finance explains that when a property is on the list of delinquent taxes and remains unpaid for four months, the county runs a title search to prepare for the notices that go out during the final 90 days before foreclosure. If the taxes still aren't paid, the county can take title, and the county sells foreclosed properties at its annual real estate tax auction.

Until the redemption deadline passes, you can generally stop the process by paying the delinquent taxes plus interest, penalties, and charges. Selling the house is one way to raise that money, as long as the sale closes before the deadline.

After the U.S. Supreme Court's 2023 decision in *Tyler v. Hennepin County*, New York amended Article 11 in 2024 to confirm that former owners can claim surplus money when a foreclosed property sells for more than what was owed. But that is a separate legal process after you've already lost the house. If you have equity, selling on your own terms is usually the better route; ask an attorney about your situation.

## What happens to back taxes when you sell for cash

A cash sale handles unpaid taxes the same way any New York closing does:

1. **Contract.** You and the buyer sign a contract, and your attorney reviews it. Let the buyer know about the back taxes up front.
2. **Title search.** The buyer's title company searches the property's tax records along with the deed, mortgages, and judgments. Unpaid taxes and tax liens show up here.
3. **Payoff figures.** The title company or attorneys get exact payoff amounts, including interest and penalties, good through the closing date. Who to get them from depends on whether the taxes are still with the town, with the county, or with the City of Poughkeepsie or Beacon.
4. **Closing.** The back taxes are paid from your proceeds at closing, along with any mortgage payoff and closing costs. Current-year taxes are usually prorated between you and the buyer.
5. **You get the rest.** Whatever remains goes to you.

You don't need the tax money in hand before you sell; in most cases the sale itself pays the taxes off. The key question is whether there's enough equity to cover the taxes, any mortgage, and other liens. If there isn't, talk to your attorney before signing anything.

* **It fits other hard situations,** like an inherited house with years of back taxes or a home also facing [mortgage foreclosure](/situations/foreclosure).

## Practical steps if you're behind on property taxes

* **Find out exactly what you owe.** Contact your town tax collector for current-year taxes, the Dutchess County Commissioner of Finance for delinquent taxes, or the city if you're in Poughkeepsie or Beacon. Ask about the redemption deadline.
* **Read every notice.** Foreclosure notices have dates on them. Keep them and share them with your attorney and any buyer.
* **Check your school tax too.** School taxes are billed separately and can be overlooked, especially on an inherited house.
* **Get an attorney involved early.** A New York real estate attorney can confirm the payoff, the deadline, and whether a sale can close in time.

Yes. Unpaid property taxes don't stop a sale. They show up in the buyer's title search and are normally paid from the sale proceeds at closing, along with interest and penalties, before the deed transfers.

### Who do I pay delinquent property taxes to in Dutchess County?

Current county and town taxes are paid to your town tax collector or receiver. Once they become delinquent, they are payable to the Dutchess County Commissioner of Finance. The cities of Poughkeepsie and Beacon collect and enforce their own taxes, so owners there should contact the city.

### How long before Dutchess County forecloses for unpaid property taxes?

It depends on the tax year and where the property is located. Dutchess County says that once a property is on the delinquent tax list and remains unpaid for four months, it runs a title search to prepare for notices sent during the final 90 days before foreclosure. Confirm your exact redemption deadline with the Commissioner of Finance or your attorney.

### Do I need to pay the back taxes before I sell?

Usually not. In most sales the back taxes are paid out of your proceeds at closing. What matters is whether the sale price covers the taxes, any mortgage, and other liens, and whether the sale closes before any foreclosure deadline.
