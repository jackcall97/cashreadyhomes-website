Losing a loved one is emotionally exhausting, and if you have been named an executor or administrator of an estate that includes real estate, the added legal and financial responsibilities can feel overwhelming. For families managing properties across the Hudson Valley—whether in Westchester, Dutchess, Orange, Putnam, Rockland, or Ulster Counties—dealing with the New York probate process is often entirely new territory. 

Selling a house during probate requires strict adherence to state laws and oversight from the local Surrogate's Court. Understanding how this process works step-by-step can help you avoid costly delays and make informed decisions for your family.

## Understanding New York Probate and Real Estate

Probate is the legal process through which a deceased person’s estate is properly administered, debts and taxes are paid, and the remaining assets are distributed to beneficiaries. When a home is tied up in an estate, it cannot simply be listed on the open market right away. The legal authority to sell must first be granted by the court.

Depending on whether the deceased left a valid will (testate) or died without one (intestate), the court will appoint either an executor or an administrator. This individual is legally responsible for managing the estate's assets, including the real property.

## Step 1: File the Probate Petition with the Surrogate's Court

The formal process begins in the county where the decedent resided at the time of death. For instance, if your relative lived in Poughkeepsie, you will deal with the Dutchess County Surrogate's Court; if they lived in White Plains, it will be Westchester County. 

The named executor must file a probate petition along with the original death certificate, the original will (if applicable), and a list of heirs and beneficiaries. Once reviewed, the court issues Letters Testamentary (or Letters of Administration if there is no will), which officially grants you the legal authority to take control of and sell the estate assets.

## Step 2: Secure and Evaluate the Property

Once you have the legal authority from the court, your next priority is securing the Hudson Valley property. Homes that sit vacant for months while going through probate are vulnerable to vandalism, weather damage, and maintenance issues. 

During this phase, you should:
* Transfer or maintain utility accounts to prevent frozen pipes or neglected landscaping.
* Secure valuable personal belongings left inside the house.
* Order a professional appraisal or market analysis to determine the accurate date-of-death value of the home, which is crucial for tax purposes.

## Step 3: Clear Debts and Understand Court Restrictions

Before any proceeds from a home sale can be distributed to heirs, the estate's debts, final utility bills, and applicable taxes must be addressed. 

It is also vital to review the language of the will. Does the will grant the executor "power of sale"? If explicit power of sale is included, the executor can generally sell the real estate without seeking explicit, separate permission for the transaction from the Surrogate's Court judge. If the will lacks this power, or if the decedent died intestate, you may need to petition the court for specific authorization to sell the property, which can add time to the timeline.
