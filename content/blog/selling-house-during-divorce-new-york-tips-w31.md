## Understanding New York Property Laws

Before putting a home on the market, it is vital to understand how real estate is handled under New York state law. 

### Equitable Distribution vs. Equal Division
New York is an "equitable distribution" state. This means that marital property—which typically includes any home purchased during the marriage regardless of whose name is on the deed—is divided fairly, though not necessarily on a strict 50/50 basis. Courts look at several factors, including the length of the marriage, each spouse's financial contributions, and future financial needs. 

### Joint Titles and Signatures
If your home is jointly titled, New York law dictates that both spouses must sign listing agreements, purchase contracts, and closing documents. If one spouse is uncooperative or refuses to sign, the process can stall indefinitely unless your divorce attorney requests a court order forcing the sale. 

## Practical Steps for Selling a Marital Home

Minimizing conflict during a divorce sale is key to preserving the net equity in your home. Here are practical ways to manage the process smoothly:

### 1. Agree on Financial Expectations Early
Arguments frequently arise over list prices, agent commissions, and how to handle lowball offers. Sit down with your respective legal counsel to establish clear ground rules. For instance, you might agree in advance to accept any verified offer within a specific percentage of a neutral appraiser's valuation.

### 2. Streamline Repairs and Preparation
Traditional home sales in the Hudson Valley often demand extensive updates, fresh paint, professional staging, and months of open houses. However, when managing the emotional and financial strain of a separation, investing thousands of dollars into home renovations is rarely practical.

## Handling the Proceeds

Once the sale is finalized, the cash proceeds should not be split haphazardly. In New York, net proceeds from the sale of a marital home are typically placed into an attorney escrow account. They remain there until a formal separation agreement or court order dictates how the funds will be divided between both parties. Always consult your divorce attorney or a certified public accountant (CPA) to understand potential capital gains exemptions and tax implications before distributing the funds.
