## What Is Probate and Why Does It Involve Real Estate?

Probate is the legal, court-supervised process of administering a deceased person’s estate. In New York State, this process is handled by the **Surrogate’s Court** in the county where the decedent resided at the time of death.

When a home is titled solely in the name of the deceased person, it cannot be sold or transferred immediately. The law requires the court to officially validate the will (if one exists) and appoint a personal representative—known as an executor or administrator—to act on behalf of the estate. Until letters testamentary or letters of administration are granted by the court, no one has the legal authority to sign a deed or sell the home.

## The New York Probate Timeline: What to Expect

Many families are shocked to learn how long probate takes in New York State. While simple, uncontested estates can take anywhere from 6 to 12 months, complex estates or those with contested wills can drag on for years. 

Here is a brief overview of the steps involved:

*   **Filing the Petition:** The named executor files a probate petition with the local Surrogate's Court (e.g., Westchester County Surrogate's Court in White Plains or Orange County Surrogate's Court in Goshen) along with the original will and death certificate.
*   **Notifying Heirs:** All distributees (legal heirs who would inherit if there were no will) must be notified or sign waivers and consents.
*   **Appointment of the Executor:** Once the court reviews the paperwork, it issues letters testamentary, granting the executor legal authority.
*   **Inventory and Valuation:** The executor must inventory estate assets, which often requires getting a professional appraisal for the Hudson Valley home.
*   **Settling Debts and Distributing Assets:** Outstanding mortgages, property taxes, and utility bills must be paid out of the estate before any final distribution can occur.

## The Hidden Costs of Holding a Probate House

During the months (or years) that a home sits tied up in probate, the financial burden falls on the estate or the heirs out-of-pocket. Hudson Valley homeowners know that carrying costs add up fast:

*   **Ongoing Mortgage Payments:** If the home still has a loan, the bank expects monthly payments regardless of the owner's passing.
*   **Property Taxes:** Towns and villages across the Hudson Valley enforce strict property tax deadlines, and late penalties accrue quickly.
*   **Utilities and Maintenance:** Keeping the heat running through harsh New York winters prevents frozen pipes, while lawn care and general upkeep are necessary to prevent code violations and municipal fines.
*   **Insurance Costs:** Standard homeowner's insurance policies often lapse or deny coverage if a property remains vacant for more than 30 days, requiring specialized vacant property insurance.
