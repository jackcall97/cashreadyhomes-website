If you are selling a house for cash in the Hudson Valley, closing looks different from many other states. New York is an **attorney-state**: residential deals are typically handled by real estate attorneys working with a title company, not by a title or escrow office alone. That holds across [Westchester](/sell-my-house-fast-westchester-county-ny), [Dutchess](/sell-my-house-fast-dutchess-county-ny), [Orange](/sell-my-house-fast-orange-county-ny), [Putnam](/sell-my-house-fast-putnam-county-ny), [Rockland](/sell-my-house-fast-rockland-county-ny), and [Ulster](/sell-my-house-fast-ulster-county-ny) Counties.

## Why New York closings center on attorneys

In many states, a title company or escrow officer can run much of a residential closing. In New York, buyers and sellers are generally each represented by their own attorney. Those attorneys negotiate the written contract, coordinate title work, prepare or review closing documents, and appear at the closing table.

Cash does not remove that structure. Paying without a mortgage can simplify the file—there is usually no lender underwriting, appraisal contingency, or mortgage recording tax on a new loan—but ownership still transfers through counsel and title. A reputable cash buyer expects attorney-and-title closings and plans for them.

## The contract of sale comes first

Under New York law, a contract for the sale of real property generally must be in writing to be enforceable. After a cash offer is accepted in principle, the next formal step is a **contract of sale** negotiated between the seller’s attorney and the buyer’s attorney.

Typical topics include purchase price, deposit (often held in escrow by one of the attorneys), what stays with the house, the quality of title the seller must deliver, permitted exceptions, and the target closing date. Financing contingencies that dominate listed sales are often lighter or absent in a true cash contract, but attorneys still add riders for tenants, probate authority, municipal issues, or as-is condition when needed.

Unlike New Jersey’s short post-signing attorney-review window, New York practice is usually to **finish attorney negotiation before anyone signs**. Once both sides execute the contract and any required deposit is delivered, the deal is generally binding under its written terms. “Attorney review” here means review and negotiation up front—not a casual cancelation period after signing.

## Title search and title insurance

The buyer’s side typically orders a **title search** through a title company. The search traces ownership and flags matters that could affect clear title: open mortgages or HELOCs, tax liens, judgments, mechanic’s liens, easements, and similar exceptions.

Clearing those items is a normal path to closing. Mortgage payoffs are arranged. Judgments or municipal charges may need to be paid or otherwise resolved. Some exceptions can be insured over; others require seller action. Your attorney and the title company coordinate that list so the buyer receives **insurable title** as the contract requires.

At closing, buyers commonly purchase an owner’s title insurance policy. Premiums in New York are regulated and based on the insured amount; exact figures depend on the policy and underwriter. Cash buyers still rely on title insurance because cash does not cure a defective chain of title.

## What closing looks like in practice

On closing day, the parties (or their attorneys, depending on custom and the deal) review final figures, sign the deed and transfer documents, and arrange disbursement of funds. The deed is then submitted for recording with the county clerk where the property sits.

Because there is no mortgage contingency on a clean cash deal, closings can often move on a schedule the seller chooses once title is clear and documents are ready. That does **not** mean every cash file closes overnight. Probate authority, payoff delays, survey questions, tenants, or code matters can still add time. A transparent buyer discusses those variables instead of promising one fixed timeline for every house.

## Transfer tax and other closing costs

New York State generally imposes a **real estate transfer tax** on conveyances when consideration exceeds a small statutory threshold. The base state rate for many residential sales is set by statute (often described as four-tenths of one percent of consideration, with a higher statutory rate for certain higher-priced transfers). The seller often pays the state transfer tax, though contracts can allocate costs differently.

Hudson Valley counties are outside New York City, so New York City’s separate transfer tax usually does not apply. Some localities may still have their own charges; your attorney will confirm what applies to your parcel. Other common line items include each side’s attorney fees, title charges, recording fees, tax or HOA adjustments, and any agreed credits. ## How this differs from a traditional listing

A listed sale and a cash sale both end with attorneys, title, a deed, and recording. What sellers feel most is upstream of the table: no lender appraisal or mortgage underwriting that can stall late; fewer repair fights driven by lender inspection requirements when the contract is clearly as-is; and a closing date negotiated around your move, estate timeline, or hardship. The New York legal spine stays the same.

## Practical tips for Hudson Valley sellers

* **Hire your own attorney early.** Even in a friendly cash deal, independent counsel should review the contract before you sign.
* **Gather documents.** Prior deeds, surveys, HOA info, leases, probate papers, and payoff statements speed title work.
* **Disclose title and occupancy issues early.** Tenants, heirs, judgments, and open permits are often solvable when they surface soon enough.
* **Confirm funds and closing partners in writing.** A serious cash buyer can show proof of funds and name the attorney and title company they intend to use.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need an attorney to sell my house for cash in New York?

Yes. In typical residential practice you should expect your own real estate attorney. New York closings are attorney-driven; cash payment does not replace legal representation or title work.

### Is there an attorney review period after I sign, like in New Jersey?

Generally no. In New York, attorneys usually negotiate the contract of sale before it is signed. Once executed (and any required deposit is delivered), the contract is typically binding under its written terms. Ask your attorney how your specific contract works.

### What does a title search look for?

A title search reviews ownership history and looks for issues that could affect clear title—such as open mortgages, tax liens, judgments, easements, or other encumbrances—so they can be cleared or addressed before closing.

### Who pays New York State transfer tax on a cash sale?

The seller often pays the state real estate transfer tax, but the contract can allocate closing costs differently. Your attorney will confirm the amounts that apply to your transaction and location.

### Can a cash buyer close without a title company?

Serious buyers work with a title company for the search, clearance, and title insurance even when there is no bank loan. Skipping title work is a red flag, not a shortcut.

### How fast can a Hudson Valley cash closing happen?

It depends on title clearance, document readiness, probate or payoff issues, and the date you choose. Cash can remove lender delays, but it does not erase every legal or recording step. A good buyer gives a realistic range for your property rather than a one-size-fits-all promise.
